🐾 Unit III: Biodiversity & Conservation

Syllabus Unit 3

3.1 Three Levels of Biodiversity

1. Genetic Diversity: Variation of genes within a single species (e.g., $>50,000$ varieties of rice and $>1,000$ varieties of mango in India). 2. Species Diversity: Variety and abundance of different species within a region (e.g., Western Ghats have higher amphibian species richness than Eastern Ghats). 3. Ecosystem Diversity: Variety of distinct habitats, biotic communities, and ecological processes (e.g., Deserts, Rain Forests, Mangroves, Wetlands, Coral Reefs, Alpine Meadows).

---

3.2 Biodiversity Hotspots & Norman Myers Criteria

A region qualifies as a Global Biodiversity Hotspot only if it fulfills two strict criteria: 1. It must contain at least 1,500 species of vascular plants as endemics ($>0.5\%$ of the world's total). 2. It must have lost at least 70% of its original primary habitat (high degree of threat).

The 4 Biodiversity Hotspots in India:

1. HIMALAYA: Entire Indian Himalayan region (including Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan, China).
2. WESTERN GHATS & SRI LANKA: Mountain range along the west coast of peninsular India.
3. INDO-BURMA: Encompasses Northeastern India (south of Brahmaputra river) and Andaman Islands.
4. SUNDALAND: Covers Nicobar group of Islands (extending into Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore).

---

3.3 Threats to Biodiversity: The HIPPO Framework

[ H ] HABITAT LOSS & FRAGMENTATION (Principal threat: deforestation, urban sprawl, dams)
[ I ] INVASIVE ALIEN SPECIES (E.g. Lantana camara, Parthenium hysterophorus, Water Hyacinth, African Catfish)
[ P ] POPULATION GROWTH (Exponential human demand for natural resource extraction)
[ P ] POLLUTION (Pesticide biomagnification, heavy metal toxicity, plastic ingestion, acid rain)
[ O ] OVEREXPLOITATION & POACHING (Illegal wildlife trade: tiger skins/bones, rhino horn, elephant ivory)

4.2 Water Pollution: BOD, COD & Eutrophication Mechanism

* BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand): Amount of dissolved oxygen ($DO$) required by aerobic microorganisms to biologically decompose organic waste in water over 5 days at 20°C. High BOD = Highly polluted water. * COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand): Amount of oxygen required to chemically oxidize all organic and inorganic compounds using strong oxidants ($K_2Cr_2O_7$). $\text{COD} > \text{BOD}$ always.

The Eutrophication Sequence:

Agricultural Fertilizer Runoff (Nitrates & Phosphates) & Untreated Sewage enter water body
                                   │
                                   ▼
          ALGAL BLOOM (Rapid proliferation of surface green algae)
                                   │
                                   ▼
        Algae form thick green carpet, blocking sunlight penetration
                                   │
                                   ▼
    Submerged aquatic plants die due to lack of photosynthesis
                                   │
                                   ▼
Decomposers (bacteria) consume dead biomass, using up all Dissolved Oxygen (DO crash)
                                   │
                                   ▼
      ANAEROBIC STATE -> Massive Fish Kill & Ecosystem Collapse (Dead Zone)

4.3 Toxicological Water Pollution Diseases & Case Studies

* Minamata Disease: Mercury ($Hg$) poisoning transformed into Methylmercury by aquatic bacteria; causes severe neurological damage (Minamata Bay, Japan 1956). * Itai-Itai Disease: Cadmium ($Cd$) poisoning from mining effluents; causes severe bone softening and renal failure (Toyama, Japan 1912). * Blue Baby Syndrome (Methemoglobinemia): Excessive Nitrates ($NO_3^-$) in drinking water converting hemoglobin into methemoglobin, inhibiting oxygen transport in infants. * Bhopal Gas Tragedy (1984): Lethal release of Methyl Isocyanate (MIC) gas from Union Carbide pesticide plant, causing over 15,000 fatalities.

---

4.4 Ozone Layer Depletion & Catalytic Chlorine Cycle

In the Stratosphere (15–30 km), the ozone layer protects life by absorbing lethal UV-B & UV-C rays. CFCs released into the stratosphere are broken down by UV radiation to release reactive Chlorine radicals ($Cl^\bullet$):

$$\text{CFC} + h\nu \longrightarrow Cl^\bullet$$ $$Cl^\bullet + O_3 \longrightarrow ClO^\bullet + O_2$$ $$ClO^\bullet + O^\bullet \longrightarrow Cl^\bullet + O_2$$

Net result: A single free chlorine radical ($Cl^\bullet$) can catalytically destroy over 100,000 ozone molecules before being deactivated! International Treaty: Montreal Protocol (1987) phased out CFCs and ozone-depleting substances worldwide.

1. Bishnois of Rajasthan (1730): Led by Amrita Devi Bishnoi; 363 Bishnoi community members sacrificed their lives hugging sacred Khejri trees (Prosopis cineraria) to prevent them from being felled by the King of Jodhpur. 2. Chipko Movement (1973): Originated in Mandal village (Chamoli, Uttarakhand); villagers led by Sunderlal Bahuguna, Chandi Prasad Bhatt, and Gaura Devi hugged forest trees to prevent commercial logging contracts; led to a 15-year ban on green felling in the Himalayas. 3. Appiko Movement (1983): Southern counterpart of Chipko in Western Ghats (Uttara Kannada, Karnataka); led by Pandurang Hegde (Appiko means "to embrace" in Kannada); saved Western Ghats biodiversity from clear-felling. 4. Silent Valley Movement (1978): Led by Kerala Sasthra Sahithya Parishad (KSSP) to save the pristine evergreen tropical rainforest of Silent Valley from being submerged by a proposed Hydroelectric Dam on the Kunthipuzha River; declared a National Park in 1984. 5. Jungle Bachao Andolan (1982): Tribal movement in Singhbhum (Bihar/Jharkhand); adivasis protested government plan to replace natural Sal forests with commercial Teak monoculture (Greed game over political game). 6. Narmada Bachao Andolan (1985 onwards): Led by Medha Patkar and Baba Amte; massive social movement protesting the displacement of hundreds of thousands of indigenous people without adequate rehabilitation due to the Sardar Sarovar Dam on the Narmada River. 7. Tehri Dam Conflict: Protest against construction of Asia's highest rockfill dam on the Bhagirathi River in Uttarakhand, highlighting massive seismic risks in earthquake Zone V and submergence of historic Tehri town.
