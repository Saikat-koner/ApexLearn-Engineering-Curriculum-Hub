* **Water Resources:** Over-extraction $\rightarrow$ Water table depletion; Salinization. *Remedies:* Rainwater harvesting, drip irrigation, watershed management.

* **Land Resources:** Soil degradation, Desertification, Salinization. *Remedies:* Terracing, crop rotation, organic farming, shelterbelts.

### 2.4 Ecological Pyramids: Upright vs Inverted
```
1. PYRAMID OF ENERGY (ALWAYS UPRIGHT in all ecosystems due to 2nd Law of Thermodynamics)
         ▲  [ Apex Predator: 10 J ]
        / \ [ Secondary Consumer: 100 J ]
       /   \ [ Primary Consumer: 1,000 J ]
      /─────\ [ Primary Producer: 10,000 J ]

2. PYRAMID OF BIOMASS
   • Terrestrial (Forest / Grassland): UPRIGHT (Huge tree biomass > herbivores > carnivores)
   • Aquatic (Ocean / Lake): INVERTED!
         /─────\ [ Fish / Whales: Large Biomass ]  ▲ (Inverted)
        /       \ [ Zooplankton ]                  │
       /         \ [ Phytoplankton: Small standing crop, rapid turnover ]
```

## 🐾 UNIT III: Biodiversity & Conservation

### 3.1 Three Levels of Biodiversity
1. **Genetic Diversity:** Variation of genes within a single species (e.g., $>50,000$ varieties of rice and $>1,000$ varieties of mango in India).
2. **Species Diversity:** Variety and abundance of different species within a region (e.g., Western Ghats have higher amphibian species richness than Eastern Ghats).
3. **Ecosystem Diversity:** Variety of distinct habitats, biotic communities, and ecological processes (e.g., Deserts, Rain Forests, Mangroves, Wetlands, Coral Reefs, Alpine Meadows).

---

### 3.2 Biodiversity Hotspots & Norman Myers Criteria
A region qualifies as a **Global Biodiversity Hotspot** only if it fulfills **two strict criteria**:
1. It must contain at least **1,500 species of vascular plants as endemics** ($>0.5\%$ of the world's total).
2. It must have lost at least **70% of its original primary habitat** (high degree of threat).

#### The 4 Biodiversity Hotspots in India:
```
1. HIMALAYA: Entire Indian Himalayan region (including Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan, China).
2. WESTERN GHATS & SRI LANKA: Mountain range along the west coast of peninsular India.
3. INDO-BURMA: Encompasses Northeastern India (south of Brahmaputra river) and Andaman Islands.
4. SUNDALAND: Covers Nicobar group of Islands (extending into Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore).
```

---

### 3.3 Threats to Biodiversity: The HIPPO Framework
```
[ H ] HABITAT LOSS & FRAGMENTATION (Principal threat: deforestation, urban sprawl, dams)
[ I ] INVASIVE ALIEN SPECIES (E.g. Lantana camara, Parthenium hysterophorus, Water Hyacinth, African Catfish)
[ P ] POPULATION GROWTH (Exponential human demand for natural resource extraction)
[ P ] POLLUTION (Pesticide biomagnification, heavy metal toxicity, plastic ingestion, acid rain)
[ O ] OVEREXPLOITATION & POACHING (Illegal wildlife trade: tiger skins/bones, rhino horn, elephant ivory)
```

#### IUCN Red List Classification & EDGE Species:
* **IUCN Categories:** Extinct ($EX$) $\rightarrow$ Extinct in Wild ($EW$) $\rightarrow$ Critically Endangered ($CR$) $\rightarrow$ Endangered ($EN$) $\rightarrow$ Vulnerable ($VU$) $\rightarrow$ Near Threatened ($NT$) $\rightarrow$ Least Concern ($LC$).
* **EDGE Species:** **E**volutionarily **D**istinct and **G**lobally **E**ndangered species with few close relatives on the tree of life (e.g., Gharial, Red Panda, Pangolin).

## 🏭 UNIT IV: Environmental Pollution & Waste Management

### 4.1 Air Pollution: Primary vs Secondary Pollutants & Smog
```
                                         AIR POLLUTANTS
                                               │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                                                           ▼
[ PRIMARY POLLUTANTS ]                                                      [ SECONDARY POLLUTANTS ]
Emitted directly from the source                                            Formed in atmosphere via chemical reactions
• Carbon Monoxide (CO: incomplete combustion)                               • Ozone (O3 in Troposphere: photochemical)
• Sulfur Dioxide (SO2: coal burning)                                        • Peroxyacetyl Nitrate (PAN: causes eye irritation)
• Nitrogen Oxides (NO, NO2: auto exhaust)                                   • Acid Rain (H2SO4, HNO3)
• Particulate Matter (PM2.5, PM10)                                          • Photochemical Smog
```

#### Classical Smog vs Photochemical Smog:
| Parameter | Classical (London) Smog | Photochemical (Los Angeles) Smog |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Climate** | Cool, humid, winter mornings | Warm, dry, sunny afternoons |
| **Key Ingredients** | Smoke, Fog, Sulfur Dioxide ($SO_2$) | $NO_x$, Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs), Sunlight, $O_3$, PAN |
| **Chemical Nature** | **Reducing Smog** (high $SO_2$) | **Oxidizing Smog** (high Ozone & PAN) |

---

### 4.2 Water Pollution: BOD, COD & Eutrophication Mechanism
* **BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand):** Amount of dissolved oxygen ($DO$) required by aerobic microorganisms to biologically decompose organic waste in water over 5 days at 20°C. High BOD = Highly polluted water.
* **COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand):** Amount of oxygen required to chemically oxidize all organic and inorganic compounds using strong oxidants ($K_2Cr_2O_7$). $\text{COD} > \text{BOD}$ always.

#### The Eutrophication Sequence:
```
Agricultural Fertilizer Runoff (Nitrates & Phosphates) & Untreated Sewage enter water body
                                   │
                                   ▼
          ALGAL BLOOM (Rapid proliferation of surface green algae)
                                   │
                                   ▼
        Algae form thick green carpet, blocking sunlight penetration
                                   │
                                   ▼
    Submerged aquatic plants die due to lack of photosynthesis
                                   │
                                   ▼
Decomposers (bacteria) consume dead biomass, using up all Dissolved Oxygen (DO crash)
                                   │
                                   ▼
      ANAEROBIC STATE -> Massive Fish Kill & Ecosystem Collapse (Dead Zone)
```

---

### 4.3 Toxicological Water Pollution Diseases & Case Studies
* **Minamata Disease:** Mercury ($Hg$) poisoning transformed into Methylmercury by aquatic bacteria; causes severe neurological damage (Minamata Bay, Japan 1956).
* **Itai-Itai Disease:** Cadmium ($Cd$) poisoning from mining effluents; causes severe bone softening and renal failure (Toyama, Japan 1912).
* **Blue Baby Syndrome (Methemoglobinemia):** Excessive Nitrates ($NO_3^-$) in drinking water converting hemoglobin into methemoglobin, inhibiting oxygen transport in infants.
* **Bhopal Gas Tragedy (1984):** Lethal release of **Methyl Isocyanate (MIC)** gas from Union Carbide pesticide plant, causing over 15,000 fatalities.

---

### 4.4 Ozone Layer Depletion & Catalytic Chlorine Cycle
In the Stratosphere (15–30 km), the ozone layer protects life by absorbing lethal UV-B & UV-C rays.
CFCs released into the stratosphere are broken down by UV radiation to release reactive Chlorine radicals ($Cl^\bullet$):

$$\text{CFC} + h\nu \longrightarrow Cl^\bullet$$
$$Cl^\bullet + O_3 \longrightarrow ClO^\bullet + O_2$$
$$ClO^\bullet + O^\bullet \longrightarrow Cl^\bullet + O_2$$

**Net result:** A single free chlorine radical ($Cl^\bullet$) can catalytically destroy over **100,000 ozone molecules** before being deactivated!
*International Treaty:* **Montreal Protocol (1987)** phased out CFCs and ozone-depleting substances worldwide.

         ▲  [ 1. REDUCE ]  (Avoid waste generation at source: Most Preferred)

## 👥 UNIT VI: Human Communities, Environmental Laws & Movements

### 6.1 Major Environmental Legislation in India

| Environmental Act & Year | Objective & Legal Scope | Key Regulatory Provisions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wildlife Protection Act, 1972** | Protection of wild animals, birds, and plants | Created 6 Schedules of protection; prohibited hunting; established National Parks & Sanctuaries; created Central Zoo Authority. |
| **Water (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act, 1974** | Prevention and abatement of water pollution | Established **CPCB (Central Pollution Control Board)** and **SPCBs (State Pollution Control Boards)**; empowers boards to set effluent standards. |
| **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980** | Check indiscriminate deforestation | Restricts diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes without prior central government approval. |
| **Air (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act, 1981** | Control and abatement of air pollution | Regulates industrial emissions; empowers SPCBs to declare air pollution control areas; **amended in 1987 to include Noise** as an air pollutant. |
| **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 (EPA)** | **Umbrella Legislation** enacted after Bhopal Gas Tragedy | Comprehensive national environmental law; authorizes central government to set environmental standards, inspect industries, and shut down violators. |
| **National Green Tribunal (NGT) Act, 2010** | Speedy disposal of environmental disputes | Established specialized environmental court (NGT); ensures expeditious environmental justice with 6-month resolution mandate. |

TIMELINE OF INDIAN ENVIRONMENTAL MOVEMENTS:

1. **Bishnois of Rajasthan (1730):** Led by **Amrita Devi Bishnoi**; 363 Bishnoi community members sacrificed their lives hugging sacred **Khejri trees** (*Prosopis cineraria*) to prevent them from being felled by the King of Jodhpur.
2. **Chipko Movement (1973):** Originated in Mandal village (Chamoli, Uttarakhand); villagers led by **Sunderlal Bahuguna**, **Chandi Prasad Bhatt**, and **Gaura Devi** hugged forest trees to prevent commercial logging contracts; led to a 15-year ban on green felling in the Himalayas.
3. **Appiko Movement (1983):** Southern counterpart of Chipko in Western Ghats (Uttara Kannada, Karnataka); led by **Pandurang Hegde** (*Appiko* means "to embrace" in Kannada); saved Western Ghats biodiversity from clear-felling.
4. **Silent Valley Movement (1978):** Led by Kerala Sasthra Sahithya Parishad (KSSP) to save the pristine evergreen tropical rainforest of Silent Valley from being submerged by a proposed Hydroelectric Dam on the Kunthipuzha River; declared a National Park in 1984.
5. **Jungle Bachao Andolan (1982):** Tribal movement in Singhbhum (Bihar/Jharkhand); adivasis protested government plan to replace natural **Sal forests** with commercial **Teak monoculture** (*Greed game over political game*).
6. **Narmada Bachao Andolan (1985 onwards):** Led by **Medha Patkar** and **Baba Amte**; massive social movement protesting the displacement of hundreds of thousands of indigenous people without adequate rehabilitation due to the **Sardar Sarovar Dam** on the Narmada River.
7. **Tehri Dam Conflict:** Protest against construction of Asia's highest rockfill dam on the Bhagirathi River in Uttarakhand, highlighting massive seismic risks in earthquake Zone V and submergence of historic Tehri town.
